Why speed and velocity tell different stories

Distance remembers the whole journey.

Distance adds every meter traveled. Displacement connects the starting point to the current point, with a direction.

s̄ = d/t   ·   v̄ = (r − r₀)/t

In 2D, Δr = (Δx, Δy). The vector's magnitude is |Δr| = √(Δx² + Δy²). Always d ≥ |Δr|, so s̄ ≥ |v̄|.

Turning changes velocity, even at constant speed.

The white arrow is the instantaneous velocity, tangent to the path. Its magnitude equals the current speed. On a circle, velocity changes direction continuously.

v = u T̂   ·   aₙ = u²/R

The slider controls tangential acceleration, which changes speed. The circular track also supplies centripetal acceleration. At t = 0, averages are undefined; a pause adds no time.
